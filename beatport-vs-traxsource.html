Beatport vs Traxsource: which store fits your sound?

They sell electronic music to working DJs, but digging on Beatport and Traxsource feels like walking into two completely different record shops. How their catalogues, audio formats, metadata, and digging cultures actually compare.

By Offbeat Editorial TeamUpdated October 20267 min read
Read the verdictAll DJ music stores

If you play electronic music, your DJ collection will almost certainly draw from Beatport, Traxsource, or both. But treating them as interchangeable is how you end up either playing the exact same Top 10 Tech House chart as every other DJ in your town or spending three hours digging through subgenres you never play.

Beatport is the commercial powerhouse: massive catalogue breadth, peak-time techno, festival EDM, drum & bass, and deep software streaming integrations. Traxsource is the boutique crate-digger: founded by house music producers (Brian Tappert and Marc Pomeroy of Soulfuric), built around label culture, and still the undisputed home for deep house, soulful grooves, and warm organic dance music.

The quick verdict: which should you dig in?

When to use Beatport

Dig on Beatport if your sets center around Techno (Peak Time, Raw, Melodic), Tech House, Drum & Bass, Trance, or Mainstage Club. It is also the necessary choice if you rely on AIFF file downloads for flawless CDJ metadata or use Beatport Streaming inside rekordbox, Serato DJ Pro, or Engine DJ.

When to use Traxsource

Dig on Traxsource if you play Deep House, Soulful House, Jackin' House, Nu-Disco, Afro House, or Classic Underground. If you want sets that sound sophisticated, soulful, and distinct from cookie-cutter festival playlists, Traxsource’s label-first charts and DJ charts will uncover gems you will never find on Beatport’s main pages.

The metadata dilemma: AIFF vs WAV for club DJs

One of the most consequential differences between the two stores is file format support for lossless audio. If you play on Pioneer CDJ-3000s or standalone systems using USB thumb drives prepared in rekordbox, metadata retention is crucial.

  • Why AIFF on Beatport is gold: AIFF delivers uncompressed, studio-grade lossless PCM audio (identical in sonic resolution to WAV), but unlike WAV, AIFF supports full ID3 metadata chunks. When you purchase an AIFF from Beatport, it arrives with embedded high-res cover art, release year, genre, artist, and key tags. When exported to USB for a CDJ, your waveforms, artwork, and search tags never break.
  • The WAV limitation on Traxsource: Traxsource sells lossless audio in WAV format only (no AIFF option). While WAV sounds pristine, the WAV header standard lacks universal ID3 tagging. If you move WAV files between DJ apps or rebuild your database, metadata can easily get stripped or fail to display properly on CDJ screens. House DJs who buy WAVs on Traxsource usually run them through a tag converter (or convert to AIFF in Apple Music / XLD) before importing into rekordbox.

Pricing & subscription costs in 2026

Both stores follow a similar à la carte pricing structure for permanent digital file purchases, but their subscription offerings differ completely:

Standard MP3s (320 kbps)$1.49 to $1.99 per track on both platforms. Exclusive new releases are priced at $2.49 for the first 2–4 weeks.
Lossless Audio Upgrade+$0.75 per track surcharge on both stores. Buying 20 lossless AIFF tracks on Beatport costs roughly $45–$55 total.
Beatport Streaming (Core $14.99 / Pro $29.99/mo)Beatport offers native in-app streaming for Serato, rekordbox, Traktor, djay, and Engine DJ. The Pro tier lets you cache up to 1,000 tracks locally in your offline locker for club sets without internet.
Traxsource Download-Only FocusTraxsource does not offer a streaming service. Every purchase is a permanent file download to your hard drive, supporting true library ownership.

Digging strategy: how to find tracks that stand out

The biggest trap for new DJs on Beatport is shopping exclusively on the Main Top 100. If a Tech House track is sitting at #3 on Beatport, three other DJs playing the same club tonight already bought it. Here is how working DJs dig effectively on each platform:

  1. On Beatport: Use the Hype 100 and Label Feeds: The Beatport Hype charts filter out major label juggernauts and highlight smaller, independent labels pushing cutting-edge sounds. Follow specific boutique labels (like Innervisions, Solid Grooves Raw, Drumcode, Hospital Records, or Rekids) and check their new catalog additions rather than the main store frontpage.
  2. On Traxsource: Dig via Weekend Weapons and DJ Charts: Traxsource’s weekly "Weekend Weapons" and "Essential Cuts" are curated by seasoned house music A&Rs. Browse guest charts from respected selectors (like Louie Vega, Kerri Chandler, or Jimpster) to uncover soulful B-sides and unreleased dubs.
  3. Don't overlook Bandcamp: For indie techno, UK bass, breakbeat, and underground house, always cross-reference Bandcamp. Artists receive an average of 80–85% of revenue on Bandcamp (and 93% on Bandcamp Fridays), making it the most artist-supportive platform for underground releases.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Beatport or Traxsource better for house music DJs?

Traxsource is superior for Deep House, Soulful House, Jackin' House, Nu-Disco, and Afro House, curated by DJs who run underground labels. Beatport is stronger for peak-time Tech House, Minimal/Deep Tech, and mainstage club tracks.

Why do DJs prefer AIFF files from Beatport over WAV from Traxsource?

Both AIFF and WAV offer uncompressed 16-bit or 24-bit 44.1kHz lossless audio quality, but AIFF natively supports full ID3 metadata tags and embedded album artwork. When exported to USB drives for Pioneer CDJs or rekordbox, AIFF tracks always retain artist, key, and artwork, whereas standard WAV metadata can drop.

How much do tracks cost on Beatport vs Traxsource in 2026?

Standard 320 kbps MP3s cost $1.49 to $1.99 on both stores, with exclusive new releases priced up to $2.49. Beatport charges a $0.75 lossless fee per track for WAV or AIFF downloads, while Traxsource charges a flat $0.75 upgrade fee per track for WAV.
