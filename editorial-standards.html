Editorial policy

How we research, and what we will not claim

Offbeat is written for DJs who would rather know what a guide is based on than read another confident-sounding listicle. This page is the short version of our rules: what we check, how we date it, how we make money, and how to get something corrected.

By Offbeat Editorial TeamReviewed October 20264 min read
About OffbeatSend a correction

What our recommendations are based on

Every buying page is built from the same four inputs, in this order:

  1. Official documentation. Manufacturer spec sheets, software support pages and licence terms. If a feature is not documented by the company that makes it, we do not treat it as a fact.
  2. Compatibility, confirmed. Which software edition a controller unlocks, whether a licence transfers, and which operating systems are supported. This is the detail that most often decides a purchase and the one most often left out of round-ups.
  3. Retail listings, dated. Prices come from current listings, with the month they were checked written on the page. Retail prices move; a price without a date is decoration.
  4. Workflow judgement. Whether a thing is actually usable in the way a DJ works — a short pitch fader is cheap until you try to beatmatch on it. This part is opinion, and we label it as opinion.

What we do not do

  • We do not invent hands-on testing. When a page says "reviewed", it means we have compared the documented specifications, software support, price and workflow fit. If we ever handle a unit for a specific page, the page says what we tested and how long for. There is no "after 40 hours with the FLX10" line anywhere on this site unless someone actually did that.
  • We do not publish invented scores. No star ratings out of nowhere, no "8.7/10" arrived at by vibes. Where a page has a verdict, it is a sentence about who should buy the thing and who should not.
  • We do not sell rankings. A product can be criticised, or left out, even when it would earn us a commission. Several current guides recommend the cheaper option over the one with the better affiliate programme.
  • We do not use vague urgency. No countdown timers, no "only three left", no invented limited offers. Retailers do that well enough on their own.
  • We do not republish other people's reviews as research. Vendor pages, support documentation and retailer listings are sources. Another affiliate site's list is not.

Images

Most photography on this site is editorial illustration: it shows a type of setup, not the specific product being reviewed. Those images are captioned as such, because a stock photo of someone else's controller presented as "the unit we tested" is a small lie that makes everything else on the page less believable. Where a page carries a photograph of a specific product, it is credited, and we use it only when we can confirm what it shows and under what licence.

How we make money

Some links to retailers are affiliate links. If you buy through one, the retailer pays us a small percentage, and the price you pay does not change. Every page with affiliate links carries a disclosure at the top, and the links are marked as sponsored where the markup allows it.

Two practical consequences. First, we prefer to link to a specific product page rather than a search results page, because a link that dumps you into a retailer's search results is a commission attempt disguised as a recommendation. Second, when the honest advice is "don't buy anything yet", the page says that — several of our guides open by telling you to keep your money.

Dates, updates and corrections

Pages carry the month they were last reviewed. Prices and software plans are rechecked when a guide is updated, and every price statement names its month. Software is the fastest-moving part of this site: rekordbox, Serato, Splice and the distributors all changed their pricing or terms within the last year, and a 2023 figure is worse than no figure.

If something here is wrong, tell us. We will correct the page and note what changed and when, rather than quietly editing it. Corrections are not a PR problem; a wrong price costs a reader real money.

On automation

Parts of this site were originally assembled with automated tooling, which is how it ended up with pages full of confident sentences that had no source behind them. We have rewritten or removed those pages, and the build checks now catch the specific failure modes: machine-written phrases in metadata, duplicated "how to use this guide" scaffolding, generic product blurbs and unverifiable claims. Tools are useful for structure and for catching mistakes. They are not a substitute for knowing what you are talking about, and we do not present them as one.

If a page reads like it was generated rather than written, that is a bug. Report it.
