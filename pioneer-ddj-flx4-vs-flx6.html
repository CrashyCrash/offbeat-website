DDJ-FLX4 vs DDJ-FLX6-GT

Same software, same brand, roughly $200 apart. The question is whether you need four channels — and whether the FLX6's extra controls help or get in the way while you are still learning.

These two controllers share a family and a software package: both run rekordbox and Serato DJ Pro, both are bus-powered, and both have the same jog-wheel style. The difference is what sits around the jogs. The FLX4 is a two-channel deck with exactly the controls you need to learn on. The FLX6-GT adds two more channels, larger jogs, a merge-fx section and a set of scratch tools.

That makes this less a "which is better" question and more a "which mistake can you afford" question. Buy the FLX6 too early and you pay for controls you will not use for a year. Buy the FLX4 and start playing four-deck sets immediately and you will be shopping again in months.

The short answer

Buy the DDJ-FLX4 if

  • This is your first controller and you are learning to blend two tracks.
  • You want the smallest, lightest deck that still feels like real gear.
  • You would rather spend the difference on headphones or music.

Buy the DDJ-FLX6-GT if

  • You already mix confidently and want four channels for layering or acapellas.
  • You play parties where you might need to run a second source.
  • The larger jogs and merge-fx are features you will actually reach for.

Both are two-channel audio interfaces in terms of physical outputs; the FLX6's extra channels are mixed internally. If you need to plug in a second sound source, that is a different class of controller.

What the extra $200 actually buys

Two more decks. The FLX6's fourth channel is not a gimmick: it lets you run loops, acapellas or a third track without swapping what is already playing. If you play open-format sets, or you like layering percussion over a track you are already mixing out of, you will use it.

Bigger jogs. The difference in feel is real, and it matters most for scratching. If you plan to learn turntablism, starting on larger platters saves a transition later.

Merge-fx and scratch tools. These are genuinely useful and genuinely confusing at first. On the FLX4 you learn to mix; on the FLX6 you learn to mix while a pad is doing something you did not intend.

Weight. The FLX6 is heavier and larger. If you are carrying a controller to a friend's flat every other weekend, the FLX4 is the one you will actually take.

Who each one is for

  • First controller, bedroom and small parties: FLX4. Nothing on the FLX6 will make you a better DJ in your first year.
  • Second controller, already gigging: FLX6-GT. Four channels solve a real problem once you are playing longer sets.
  • Scratch focus: FLX6-GT, or better, save for a motorised unit — see our Rane One review.
  • Club aspirations: either, but spend the difference on rekordbox time. The booth will not care which one you practised on.

One more consideration: the FLX4's successor cycle is not a reason to wait. Controllers in this range hold their usability for years, and the software support is shared across the family.

Frequently asked questions

Do both controllers work with Serato and rekordbox?

Yes. Both unlock Serato DJ Pro and run rekordbox, which is unusual at this price and one of the reasons the FLX line is easy to recommend.

Can the FLX4 run four decks in software?

The software can, but the controller has two physical channels and no dedicated controls for the extra decks. You will be switching layers rather than mixing them.

Is the FLX6 worth it for home practice?

Only if you already mix confidently. Otherwise the extra controls are a distraction, and the FLX4 leaves more budget for headphones, music and a stand.
