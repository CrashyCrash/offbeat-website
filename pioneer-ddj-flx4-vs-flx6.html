DDJ-FLX4 vs DDJ-FLX6-GT

Both support rekordbox, but their Serato entitlements and physical connections differ. Compare compact two-deck control with four software-deck controls—not nonexistent external inputs.

By Offbeat Editorial TeamReviewed October 20267 min read

Both controllers support rekordbox, but their Serato access differs: the FLX4 works with free Serato DJ Lite (Pro needs a separate license or subscription), while the FLX6-GT unlocks Serato DJ Pro when connected. The FLX4 controls two software decks and supports compatible mobile rekordbox devices; the FLX6-GT controls four software decks on a computer and adds larger jogs, Merge FX and a separate RCA booth output. Neither accepts an external CDJ, AUX player or second laptop as an audio input.

That makes this less a "which is better" question and more a "which mistake can you afford" question. Buy the FLX6 before you need four software decks and you may pay for unused controls. Choose the FLX4 for two-deck practice, but verify your software, mobile power and audio routing before buying.

The short answer

Buy the DDJ-FLX4 if

  • This is your first controller and you are learning to blend two tracks.
  • You want the smallest, lightest deck that still feels like real gear.
  • You would rather spend the difference on headphones or music.

Buy the DDJ-FLX6-GT if

  • You want four software-deck controls for layering or acapellas.
  • You need a separate RCA booth-monitor feed alongside the master output—not a second external audio source.
  • The larger jogs and merge-fx are features you will actually reach for.

Four software-deck controls do not mean four external inputs. Neither controller accepts a CDJ, AUX player or second laptop audio source. The FLX6-GT adds a separate RCA booth output; choose a mixer with line inputs if you need external sources.

What the additional controls actually buy

Two more decks. The FLX6's additional software-deck controls let you run loops, acapellas or a third track without swapping what is already playing. If you play open-format sets, or you like layering percussion over a track you are already mixing out of, you will use it.

Bigger jogs. The FLX6-GT has larger jog wheels, which may be useful if platter size matters to your technique. Neither controller has motorized platters.

Merge-fx and scratch tools. These are genuinely useful and genuinely confusing at first. On the FLX4 you learn to mix; on the FLX6 you learn to mix while a pad is doing something you did not intend.

Weight. The FLX6 is heavier and larger. If you are carrying a controller to a friend's flat every other weekend, the FLX4 is the one you will actually take.

Who each one is for

  • First controller, bedroom and small parties: FLX4. Nothing on the FLX6 will make you a better DJ in your first year.
  • Second controller, layering software decks: FLX6-GT. Four software-deck controls are useful for complex sets, but neither controller accepts external line-level players.
  • Scratch focus: FLX6-GT, or better, save for a motorised unit — see our Rane One review.
  • Club aspirations: either, but spend the difference on rekordbox time. The booth will not care which one you practised on.

One more consideration: the FLX4 supports rekordbox on compatible phones/tablets with separate 9V/3A power; USB and Bluetooth mobile modes route audio differently. Confirm the supported device, app, power and speaker path before buying.

Frequently asked questions

Is the DDJ-FLX6-GT worth it over the DDJ-FLX4?

Consider the FLX6-GT for four software-deck controls, larger jog wheels and a separate RCA booth output. Both have a microphone input; neither accepts external AUX/line/CDJ sources.

Is the DDJ-FLX4 enough for beginners?

Yes. It controls two software decks, has a microphone input and supports rekordbox on compatible phones/tablets or computers. Mobile use needs separate 9V/3A power and verified USB audio routing.

Do both controllers work with rekordbox and Serato?

Both support rekordbox. The FLX4 works with free Serato DJ Lite; Serato DJ Pro needs a separate license or subscription. The FLX6-GT unlocks Serato DJ Pro when connected.

Can I connect a CDJ or second laptop to either controller?

No. Neither has an AUX or line-level audio input for external decks or a second laptop. FLX6-GT's four channels control software decks, not physical inputs.

Does the FLX6-GT have a separate booth output?

Yes. The FLX6-GT has RCA master and separate RCA booth outputs; the FLX4 has RCA master output. Both provide headphone monitoring.

Verify the physical connections before buying

Use the official DDJ-FLX4 specifications and official DDJ-FLX6-GT specifications to confirm microphone, booth, USB, mobile power and software compatibility. Neither controller replaces a mixer with external line inputs.
