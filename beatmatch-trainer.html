Reading about beatmatching and doing it are different skills, and most people practise in the wrong order: they try to learn inside a real mix, where a wrong nudge costs them the blend. This drill separates the two. Deck A is a fixed 124 BPM loop. Deck B arrives slightly out of tempo and out of position, exactly like a record you have just dropped in. Your job is the real one.

Because the tool knows where both kicks are, it can tell you something your ears cannot yet: how far apart they were, and for how much of the drill you kept them together.

The 3-Step Real DJ Workflow

  1. Step 1: Match Pitch First. Listen to the kick rhythm. If Deck B is speeding up ahead, drag the pitch slider down. If it is lagging, drag it up. Get within 0.3 BPM before touching the platters.
  2. Step 2: Align Phase with CUE or Nudge. Tap C or the CUE button right on the downbeat of Deck A, or tap Nudge (← / →) to pull the kicks together.
  3. Step 3: Listen for the Solid Hit. When kicks align within 18ms, they fuse into one punchy transient with zero double-kick flam.

Focus the practice stage for shortcuts: Space Start/Stop · C CUE Drop · ↑↓ Pitch Fader · ←→ Nudge · 1/2 Solo

What the drill is actually training

Two separate skills, and the drill keeps them separate on purpose:

  • Tempo matching is noticing that the gap between two kicks is changing over time. It is slow feedback: you hear it over four, eight, sixteen bars.
  • Phase correction is noticing that the kicks are in the wrong place relative to each other, right now. It is fast feedback: you hear it immediately.

Almost everyone who "cannot beatmatch" is correcting phase constantly while their tempo is still wrong. The result is a permanent chase. The drill makes that visible: if the offset keeps sliding back to the same place after every nudge, the problem is the pitch fader, not your hands.

What the score means

ScoreWhat it tells youWhat to do next
85%+You are matching tempo quickly and holding phase. This is a blend that would work in a room.Turn the readouts off and repeat. Then try the hard difficulty.
65–84%You can hold a lock but lose it under small distractions.Practise releasing the nudge sooner — most corrections are too big.
40–64%Phase is being chased while the tempo is still wrong.Spend the first 20 seconds on the pitch fader only. Do not touch the nudge.
Under 40%The tempo is far off and every correction is fighting the last one.Start on easy, and move the fader in 0.05% steps until the offset stops sliding.

On real gear, a controller's pitch fader moves about 0.08% per millimetre on an ±8% range, so a 0.05% step is roughly half a millimetre. That is the size of correction the drill is asking for.

Taking it to real gear

The drill removes the two things that make real practice hard: you do not have to find two records that fit, and a mistake costs nothing. Once you can score above 80% with the readouts hidden, move to your controller and do the same thing with two copies of one track — pitch only for the first minute, then phase.

Questions about the trainer

Can you learn to beatmatch without DJ equipment?

Yes. Beatmatching is a listening skill, and this drill trains the same ear you use on a controller. What it cannot teach is the feel of a jog wheel and the monitoring workflow — that needs hardware, and the guide covers it.

Why do the two decks sound different?

So you can tell them apart while they overlap. Deck A sits in A minor, deck B a semitone above in B♭ minor, and each has a slightly different hat pattern. If they were identical you could lock them by accident and never know.

Does this work on a phone?

Yes — tap and hold the nudge buttons, and drag the pitch fader. Headphones make the detail much easier to hear, especially the flam between the two kicks.

Is anything recorded?

No. The audio is generated by your browser, the score is calculated locally, and nothing leaves the page.
