SoundCloud vs Bandcamp: one pays per play, the other per sale

These two are not competitors, which is why the comparison never resolves. SoundCloud is where music travels; Bandcamp is where it gets paid for. Here is the real 2026 fee structure, what a $10 sale is worth against 2,000 plays, and where a DJ should put a mix.

By Offbeat Editorial TeamReviewed October 20268 min read
See the mathsHow we research

Every few months someone asks which is better, and the question is a category error. SoundCloud is a network: it is where a private link to an unreleased edit travels, where a mix gets reposted, where somebody leaves a comment at 2:14. Bandcamp is a shop: it is where a stranger pays £7 for your EP and gets a lossless file and your email address in return. One is distribution, the other is a till.

That does not make the comparison useless — it makes the decision easier. You are not choosing a winner; you are deciding which of the two jobs you need done this month, and what each one costs in 2026.

The maths of a $10 sale

This is the comparison that actually matters, because it explains why the two platforms attract different kinds of artists:

ScenarioYou receiveEquivalent
$10 digital album on BandcampAbout $8 (15% share + processing)One fan who decided to pay you.
The same $10 on a Bandcamp FridayAbout $9.30–9.50The same sale, with the platform's share waived for the day.
2,000 SoundCloud playsA few dollars at most, on a monetised track2,000 strangers who heard 30 seconds and left.
2,000 Spotify streams (via a distributor or Artist Pro)Roughly $6–8 depending on territory and planAbout 700–890 hours of listening.

Read that table honestly and the strategy writes itself. If a hundred people like a track, Bandcamp turns that into several hundred dollars and a mailing list. SoundCloud turns the same hundred people into a number that grows slowly and might lead somewhere in two years. Neither is wrong — but only one of them pays for the next piece of gear.

Which is why the artists who do well use both in a specific order: SoundCloud creates the moment, Bandcamp captures it.

Where a DJ should put things

DJs are a special case on both platforms, because the thing you make most often — a mix — is the thing neither platform can legally host at scale. Here is the division that works:

  • Mixes and radio shows → SoundCloud (or Mixcloud). Private links for promoters, public uploads for the scene, comments for the people who actually listened. Understand the risk: a mix full of other people's records can be muted or taken down by content matching, and repeating it will cost you the account.
  • Your own productions → Bandcamp plus a distributor. Bandcamp for the people who want to own it, Spotify and Apple for the people who will only ever stream it. That is a two-step process, not a choice.
  • Edits and bootlegs → nowhere public. A bootleg is a derivative work. It can travel as a private link to a DJ you trust; putting it on Bandcamp or distributing it is how people lose catalogues. Read how to promote a mix for the version of this that is safe.
  • Sample packs, stems, DJ tools → Bandcamp. Buyers get a file, you get paid, no streaming economics involved. It is the only mainstream platform where selling 40 copies of something is worth doing.

One more DJ-specific note: Bandcamp is now a serious digging tool. Plenty of small club labels put the only purchasable copy of a track there, which means the same platform you sell on is also where you should be buying. That is not a coincidence; it is the same economics.

Bandcamp Friday is a schedule, not a strategy

On eight Fridays in 2026 — February 6, March 6, May 1, August 7, September 4, October 2, November 6 and December 4 — Bandcamp waives its revenue share for 24 hours. It is a genuinely good deal and it is not a business plan.

What works: have the release, the vinyl repress or the bundle already uploaded and announced days earlier, so people arrive on the day to buy something that exists. What does not: uploading at 3 pm on the day and hoping the algorithm notices a shop with four followers. The platform's own reporting puts the programme at over $154 million paid to artists since 2020 — an average of a few dollars per artist per Friday, unless you brought an audience with you.

Verdict

Start on SoundCloud if

You are building a scene, not a shop

Your output is mixes, edits, half-finished ideas and collaborations, and the goal is that other DJs know your name. $99 a year for Artist Pro also happens to be one of the cheapest ways to get your own tracks onto Spotify, with 100% of those royalties. Accept that it will not pay you much directly.

Start on Bandcamp if

You have finished music to sell

A three-track EP, a vinyl run, a pack of tools, or a catalogue you want to keep control of. There is no subscription, no algorithm, and no takedown risk for material you own. You keep about 80% of each sale, and the email addresses are yours — which is the part that compounds.

If you can only do one thing this month and you have nothing released: put a mix on SoundCloud, because it is free to try and it creates the moment you can later sell from. If you have finished tracks sitting in a folder, put them on Bandcamp first — a shop with three items in it beats a stream that has not shipped.

Before you upload: five checks

Both platforms are agreements, not just upload buttons. These are the terms worth reading once so you never have to read them in a panic:

Do you own the recordingEvery sample, vocal and loop needs to be cleared. "It was free" is not a licence.
Who can take it downContent matching can mute or remove an upload. Know how to dispute it and what evidence you need.
What you keep per saleCompare the platform share, processing and payout thresholds before pricing a release.
How fans reach youEmail capture is the asset. Bandcamp gives it to you; SoundCloud followers are borrowed attention.
What happens if you leaveCan you export the catalogue, keep the URLs alive and redirect old links?

Frequently Asked Questions

Which pays artists more, SoundCloud or Bandcamp?

Per listener reached, Bandcamp — by a wide margin. A $10 digital sale nets roughly $8 after Bandcamp's 15% share and payment processing; a few thousand SoundCloud plays pay a few dollars. Per person reached, SoundCloud is far cheaper, because listening is free.

Can I put DJ mixes on Bandcamp?

Only mixes you have the rights to. Bandcamp is a store, and a mix of other people's records sold or given away there is a rights problem, not just a formatting one. Clearance-free mixes belong on SoundCloud, Mixcloud or your own site — see our guide to publishing a mix.

Does SoundCloud pay for plays on the platform?

On Artist or Artist Pro, yes — SoundCloud monetises eligible tracks through fan-powered royalties, where a listener's subscription is shared among the artists they actually played. Rates are low, in the fractions of a cent per play, so it is pocket money until your audience is large.

Do I still need a distributor if I use SoundCloud Artist Pro?

No, that is the point of it: Artist Pro distributes to Spotify, Apple Music and 60+ other services, and since late 2025 SoundCloud keeps none of those royalties. The Artist plan at $39/year is capped at two distributed tracks a month, so check the cap before you plan a release schedule.

Is Bandcamp Friday still a thing in 2026?

Yes. Bandcamp scheduled eight Fridays in 2026 — February 6, March 6, May 1, August 7, September 4, October 2, November 6 and December 4 — when its revenue share is waived for 24 hours. Payment processing still applies, so you keep roughly 93–95% instead of about 80%.

Where should a DJ start?

With the platform that matches the thing you made. Mixes and experiments go to SoundCloud; finished productions, edits you own and anything you want to sell go to Bandcamp, with distribution to streaming handled by a distributor or by Artist Pro.
